# BLACKOUT PROTOCOL — design

Desktop top-down military action, isolated from the other NEON ARCADE games. Nine campaign operations in three acts, an endless HOLDOUT mode, seven more combat modes (team deathmatch, arms race, uplink, hunted, breach, extraction and flow trials), a live-fire range, an armory economy and four difficulty tiers.

## Stack decision

Phaser 3.90.0 (MIT), vendored locally: sprite atlases, layered game objects, render textures, camera bounds/look-ahead/shake, asset preloading, scaling and pooled sound voices. A pure CommonJS/browser simulation (`core.js`) owns collisions, AI and combat at 120 Hz, so every mechanic is testable without a browser. `missions.js` holds level data, `world.js` the weapon/enemy/gear tables, `meta.js` the economy, `save.js` the versioned save. No backend, bundler or runtime CDN.

## Modes

- **Campaign** — nine operations in three acts (I · The Fall, II · The Hunt, III · The Truth), unlocked in order from the operations board; see CAMPAIGN_PLAN.md. Each has a briefing, recommended equipment (with a buy button), a difficulty choice and a loadout. Checkpoints resume a run; dying keeps the checkpoint and counts against the NO DEATHS medal.
- **HOLDOUT** — endless killhouse waves (unlocked by finishing THE CRASH). Waves get harder in three ways at once: more hostiles on the field (8 at a time on wave 1, up to 18), a threat level that rises every three waves and makes every hostile smarter (cover, then flanking and grenades for campers, suppression and pushing reloads, then hunting and flashbangs), and new hostiles on a schedule, each announced with a NEW THREAT card and a tip (medic, mortar team, spotter drone, juggernaut, engineer, jammer…). Every fifth wave is a **red wave** with a siren, red screen edges and a boss: commander, Warden (a barrier that breaks under fire, missile salvos), the Havoc tank (front armour, a weak rear, a telegraphed main gun), the Wraith, then two at once. Later waves bring veterans with better guns, spawn from two or three approaches, and their last stragglers come for you. A named modifier (Blackout, Armoured, Swarm, Marksmen) rides every third wave that is not red; some waves drop a supply uplink to secure under fire; a clean wave pays a bonus. After each wave three crates parachute in (graded by colour, a hologram of what they sell): basics, stims, gadgets, guns for the run, a sentry, fortifications, hollow points, a partner kit, a field support charge, rebuilt cover, a thermal scan. Before a red wave a fourth, **legendary** crate holds the answer to its boss (M32 against the commander, ARC-7 against the Warden, AT4 against the tank, M110 against the Wraith). The death screen says what killed you and how to beat it; first-time milestones (waves 5, 10, 15…) pay a bonus. Seven killhouses: the default one and one per campaign operation, which pay ×1.1 to ×1.6 and keep their own best wave per difficulty.
- **Team deathmatch** — Team Alpha against Team Delta on any killhouse map, up to four operators a side, players or AI in any mix (1v1, 1v3, 2v2, 4v4...). A kill is a point for the killer's team; first to the kill limit the host picks (5, 10, 15, 20, 30, 50 or 75) wins, or whoever is ahead when the clock runs out. Everyone redeploys six seconds after going down, at their own base, or out of the other team's sight if that base is being watched. The match clock grows with the map (larger maps run longer, up to 20 minutes); the lobby tags each map small or large, small for 1v1 to 2v2 and large for 3v3 and 4v4.
  - **Every operator is whole.** Each one, player or AI, on either team, has the full move set (sprint, slide, roll, dive, vault, knife), two guns, a lethal, a tactical, stims, three perks and field support, from their own loadout. Permanent upgrades, weapon mastery and kit parts are left at the door so everyone starts level.
  - **Operator against operator.** A hit does 70% of its damage (so a shotgun at arm's length still kills) and there is no protection after it, so every pellet of a blast lands; only a dodge, a vault or the 2.5 seconds after redeploying (given up by firing) saves you. A knife kills outright. A stun (flashbang, tear gas, EMP, blackout) stops an operator firing and halves their speed for at most 2.2 seconds. Nothing of yours harms your own team; your own grenades can still kill you, which scores for the other side.
  - **Gadgets and support take sides.** A sentry, hound, seeker, mine or fire belongs to whoever put it there and goes for the other team; an EMP or blackout takes the other team's devices down; a trophy system stops their grenades. The other team's grenades and rounds are drawn in red, and their field support is announced ("ENEMY AIRSTRIKE"). The host can switch field support off for a match.
  - **AI operators** are the same operators run by `botInput`, with a frag and a stim they use. They patrol the map's points, hunt only an enemy they have seen or heard (a shot is heard for 1.5 seconds within 700 units), and peek from cover when pressed, rather than knowing where everyone is. "Enemy pressure" becomes **AI skill**: the share of a player's damage they do (40%, 60%, 80%, 100%) and how fast they react.
  - **What you see:** your team's rings and name tags are blue, the other team's red, your own amber. The minimap shows your team always, and an enemy only while they are firing or marked by your recon or your team's sensor darts. A kill feed lists who got whom and with what.
  - **Pay** is per player, on their own machine: half a bounty (25 CR, more in a chain) for each kill, and a result bonus (300 win / 150 draw / 100 loss) scaled down for a kill limit under 30 or a smaller team opposite. Only a match to 20 or more against a team at least the size of your own is kept in the record, counts for the daily and for the "win five" challenge. The AI skill multiplier applies only if there was AI on the other team.
- **Arms race** (versus) — every kill moves you a gun up a ladder of twelve; the last step is the knife, and the first knife kill on it wins for the team. A knife kill sets the victim back a step. No lethals, no field support.
- **Uplink** (versus) — three relays placed between the bases. Standing in one with nobody from the other team in it turns it your way over four seconds (faster with more of you); each relay held scores a point a second; first to 150.
- **Hunted** (versus, one against up to four) — Delta's first seat is the hunter: cloaked unless it fires, is hit, comes within 170 or is caught by the sweep every 20 seconds; a roll is a blink; a marksman rifle and a hand cannon; health for the whole squad. Nobody redeploys. The squad wins by killing it or lasting four minutes.
- **Breach** (versus, rounds) — 20 seconds of setup for the defenders with the attackers held at their base, then two minutes to plant a charge at the site (hold interact four seconds) or wipe the other side; the defenders win by wiping the attackers, running the clock out, or defusing a planted charge (five seconds). One life a round; first to four; sides swap after three. Walls are rebuilt each round.
- **Match mutators** — Night Ops, Sidearms Only and Scarcity can be added to any versus match by its host.
- **Extraction** (co-op, one or two plus an optional AI partner) — five supply caches on a killhouse map and a landing zone at the far end. Patrols grow with time and with every cache taken (Holdout's wave composer), with one boss at most. Each cache is worth more than the last; reaching the zone calls the bird, which needs 25 seconds held. Die and the loot is lost; bounties are kept.
- **Flow trials** (solo) — a seeded course of ten gates and seven practice targets through a killhouse: each map's own course and one a day. Score = time + 3 s per target missed − 0.5 s per perfect flow. The best run on a course is saved as its input record and raced as a ghost on the next attempt.
- **Training range** — every weapon and gadget, never touches progress.

All the combat modes share one lobby (the COMBAT MODES card on the main menu): the mode in force is shown beside ALL MODES, which opens a picker grouped into co-op and versus; map, enemy pressure and loadout are picked there, and it reopens as it was left. Holdout is one operator, or two with a friend (LAN or online) or an AI teammate in the open slot. Team deathmatch shows eight seats, four for Alpha and four for Delta; it opens as you and seven AI. The lobby leader adds or removes the AI in any seat and sets the kill limit and field support; anyone can move to a seat with no player in it, changing places with an AI that was there. Up to seven more players join over a LAN and are seated on the team with fewer players. A match can start once each team has someone in it and every player who has joined is ready; a player who drops out mid-match is replaced by an AI. Leaving a hosted or joined lobby (LEAVE LOBBY, Escape or B) ends that session and returns you to your own holdout lobby, where you can host again or add an AI teammate; MAIN MENU from your own lobby goes to the main menu.

**AI teammates in holdout** are squadmates whose controls are worked by the simulation instead of a person (`botInput` in `core.js`). They take a moment to react, aim at a human turning rate with some wander, fire in bursts, deal 50% damage, reload by themselves and never run dry. They play their own game inside a leash: they cover the side you are not facing, go after hostiles already in the fight (those closing on you, and the dangerous few - rocketeers, marksmen, medics, spotters, mortars, jammers - first), fight from peek spots behind low cover when under fire, fall back to cover and stim when hurt, throw a frag into a pack or behind a wall and a flashbang when rushed (never near you), set down a sentry once their kills have earned one, call out what they see, and fetch your dog tags and revive you. They roll out of a telegraphed shot about to land on them. The squad order key (T, or left stick click) cycles FREE / FOLLOW / HOLD HERE. Every squadmate, AI or a friend's, rolls, dives and slides by the player's own rules, with the same stamina costs and the same moment of a roll or dive that nothing can hit; a vault is the one move a squadmate does not have. Their kills are not yours: no combo, mastery or kill count, and half a bounty. A LAN squadmate keeps the leaner guns-and-knife kit.

## Difficulty

Story, standard and high intensity keep their original damage (×.55 / ×1 / ×1.22) and cadence. **Insane** is the skill tier:

- every authored soldier gets a reinforcement beside it (marksmen are replaced by mission specialists so sniper pairs don't decide fights), and waves get extra spawns;
- incoming damage ×1.5, post-hit protection .2s instead of .32s (lowered from ×1.85 and .14s after the October 2026 audit found a difficulty wall; the tactical rules below are what make it Insane);
- riflemen fire 3-round bursts; enemy fire cadence ×.62, wind-up ×.8, bullet speed ×1.3, aim leads 60% of your movement;
- every alerted enemy hunts along a breadth-first path field instead of waiting at your last-seen corner;
- enemies keep suppressing your last position and lob grenades at a player who stays within 110 units of one spot for 3.2s;
- fewer drops, no director medkits, 55-health checkpoint floor.

Everything stays telegraphed (wind-up lines, red sniper lasers, grenade rings, damage-direction arcs), so death comes from position and timing, not hidden rolls. Rewards scale ×.6 / ×1 / ×1.4 / ×2.2; lowering difficulty mid-mission pays at the lowest tier played.

## Combat loop and movement

**Bootcamp** (13 steps: move, sprint, slide, vault, perfect flow, roll, dive, fire, reload, swap, knife, dodge a telegraphed shot, gadget) is offered on the operations board until done and can be replayed from there. THE CRASH teaches the red laser, grenade rings and frags with stage hints, two grenadiers and a marked SPRINT · SLIDE · VAULT lane.

Unchanged from the opening slice: 250/360 run/sprint, slide → vault PERFECT FLOW, roll/dive invulnerability windows, three-hit knife combo, heavy knife, stims that damage interrupts. Fixes and additions:

- Reload and stim timers now settle at exactly zero. Before, a tiny negative remainder still read as "reloading", which blocked sprint (and slowed movement and later reloads) until a melee or roll reset it.
- Two weapon slots (primary + secondary), toggled with X / mouse wheel / controller **Y**, or selected with 1 / 2.
- Firing an empty magazine starts a reload.
- Controller X interacts or vaults when something is in reach, otherwise reloads; D-pad up is a dedicated reload, D-pad left injects a stim, D-pad right switches the selected gadget and RB deploys it. D-pad down is unbound because rocker pads report it by accident while right is pressed.

## Weapons, drops and loadouts

The first seventeen guns: ten primaries (MK18, M870, MP7, SVK-9, KV-15, M110, M249, AS-12, M32, XR-9 pulse, ARC-7 rail) and six secondaries (P226, VZ-61, .44, sawed-off, M79, AT4). Enemies carry real guns that improve across the campaign (`map.guns`). Killing a carrier of a gun you don't own always drops it; walking over it unlocks it permanently, interact swaps it into its slot on the spot. Enemies also drop ammo, health, armor, lethal or tactical refills by type. Weapon mastery (20/60/150 kills) adds reserve ammo, faster reloads and then damage.

**Roles and traits.** Every gun has a role (GENERALIST, RUN AND GUN, PRIORITY TARGETS, SUPPRESSION, SHIELD BREAKER…) and most have a trait the simulation honours: armour-piercing, counter-shot (×1.5 on a hostile winding up), suppressing (slower wind-ups, wider enemy spread, cover users duck), steady on the move (+25% from a slide or just after a flow), shield overload. The armory shows each gun's numbers and compares it with the one equipped. The roles, the changed numbers and a matrix of every gun are in `docs/BLACKOUT_BALANCE.md`; `node blackout/qa/weapon-report.cjs` regenerates the matrix.

Gadgets: lethal (frag, incendiary, proximity mine) and tactical (flashbang, smoke, EMP, holo decoy, deployable cover). Smoke blocks enemy sight; flash stuns anyone who can see the blast; EMP destroys drones, blinds sentries/armour and drops shields and barriers; the decoy draws fire; cover drops a 280-hp vaultable barrier.

## The second wave: guns and gadgets with a mechanic each

Five more primaries, each with a rule of its own: the **FR-9 burst rifle** (three rounds a pull), the **RX-4 ricochet carbine** (rounds come off a wall twice), the **M2 flamethrower** (a short stream that passes through a crowd and leaves the ground burning), the **M134 minigun** (0.7 seconds of spin-up, then 200 rounds) and the **TESLA-3 arc caster** (the bolt jumps to two more hostiles for 60% each and stalls machines). That makes 22 guns.

Lethals: **throwing knives** (one drops most soldiers, and the knife lies where it lands to be picked up again), the **cluster grenade** (one blast, then five bomblets), **C4** (planted, set off by pressing again, one charge down at a time) and the **seeker** (a rolling charge that hunts for eight seconds). Tacticals: the **grapple hook** (a fast dive along the line of aim that counts as a flow), the **sensor dart** (marks hostiles near it for twelve seconds), **tear gas** (soldiers in it cannot fight; machines and bosses are untroubled), the **trophy system** (knocks down hostile grenades, shells and rockets near it for twenty seconds) and the **ammo box**.

Four more field support calls: **supply drop** (600), **reinforcement** (1,000; a rifleman at Rook's side for 45 seconds), **gunship** (1,400; circles him for 20 seconds firing over walls) and **blackout** (1,600; a map-wide EMP that fells drones, darkens machines and blinds every soldier for five seconds).

Of these the flamethrower, minigun, C4, grapple hook and blackout are challenge awards (25 kills with fire, wave 12 in holdout, 40 explosive kills, 25 perfect flows, all six operations finished); the rest are sold. Anything the player cannot have yet, because its rank is ahead of them or its challenge is unmet, is drawn in the armory and the pickers as a silhouette in its grade colour beside what it asks for.

## Field support, perks and challenges

**Field support** is one ability carried into a run and called in (V, or BACK on a controller) when its meter is full. The meter fills with Rook's own score: a kill is worth its bounty, a flow 25, a cleared wave 60, an objective 150. It fills at ×1 in holdout, ×1.6 in team deathmatch and ×0.6 in the campaign, and starts again from empty after every call. Kills the support itself makes do not charge it.

- **Recon sweep** (450) marks every hostile on the map and in view for 20 seconds; cloaks and darkness hide nothing.
- **Sentry turret** (800) is set in front of Rook and shoots what it can see for 30 seconds.
- **K9 unit** (900) is a combat hound that paths to the nearest hostile and bites until it drops, for 25 seconds.
- **Airstrike** (1,200) lays six shells along the line of aim, starting at the aim point and walking away from Rook, each with a ring and a second's warning. It hurts him as much as any grenade if he stands in it.

Hostiles do not target the sentry or the hound; their time limit is what ends them. Their kills are Rook's (bounty, combo, kill count).

**Perks** are one per slot in three slots, survival, combat and movement, which open at ranks 2, 5 and 8. They change how a run plays rather than raising a number, which is what the permanent upgrades are for. Survival: Field Dressing (health climbs back to 60 after six seconds unhurt), Flak Vest (blasts and fire do 40% less), Second Wind (one killing blow per run leaves 30 health). Combat: Scrounger (a gun kill returns a quarter magazine to reserve; every third gadget kill returns a lethal), Ambusher (+35% damage to hostiles that have not noticed you, or that cannot fight back: flashed, suppressed or stunned), Executioner (a knife kill gives back 20 health; the knife finishes anyone under 55 health). Movement: Low Profile (hostiles must be a quarter closer to spot you), Flow State (a perfect flow gives three seconds of +30% damage; each kill in it adds a second, never more than six ahead), Hardline (support charges 25% faster; gadget kills count double).

Some are bought with credits at their tier's rank; the others are never sold and are awarded by a **career challenge**, shown with its progress on the item's card: 400 hostiles (K9 unit), two operations without dying (Second Wind), 30 knife kills (Executioner), 50 perfect flows (Flow State), five deathmatch wins (Hardline). An award is made at the end of the run that met the challenge and shown on the debrief.

In the campaign and holdout only the lobby leader carries field support and perks (a holdout squadmate has the guns-only kit). In team deathmatch every operator has their own.

## Challenges, mutators and records

- **Seeded challenges** (main menu: the CHALLENGES button beside the daily contracts opens a panel from the right with both in full - map, kit, twist, rules, your standing, PLAY and the local board; the Holdout lobby stays clear of them): a **daily** (standard pressure; only the first attempt counts, later ones are practice) and a **weekly** (high intensity; the best attempt counts). Everyone gets the same map, loadout, twist and wave seed on the same day or week (`challengeFor` in `meta.js`). The run is fair: the given loadout only, no armory upgrades or perks. Score = wave × 1000 + kills × 10 + best combo × 25. Results go on a **local board** (top ten per kind, this machine only; there is no online leaderboard). A challenge run pays as a holdout run but stays out of the ordinary holdout records and milestones.
- **Mutators** (on a cleared operation's briefing): Night Ops (dark map, ×1.2 pay), Veterans (high-intensity tactics at your difficulty's damage, ×1.25), Sidearms Only (both slots secondaries, no primary pick-ups, ×1.3), Scarcity (half reserves and drops, ×1.2). They multiply together. Not in team deathmatch or training.
- **Operation challenges**: one per operation, paid once (400 to 700 CR): three flows in THE CRASH, inside par in NO SAFE GROUND, a ×6 combo in DEAD FREQUENCY, under 40 damage in UNDERGROUND, three knife kills in IRON CONVOY, five flows and no deaths in GHOST SIGNAL.
- **S rating** needs a score of 9,800 and at least one perfect flow; the debrief says what a missed S lacked.

## Intel

The armory's INTEL tab holds a file on each of the 25 hostiles a player can meet (`World.intel`; the three unused soldier types and the team deathmatch operator have none). Files are graded on the armory's own five colours by threat: grey LOW THREAT (rifleman, rusher, scout, drone), green MODERATE (breacher, grenadier, shield, medic, bomb drone, engineer, spotter drone), blue DANGEROUS (marksman, machine gunner, mortar team, jammer, sentry gun, ghost), gold ELITE (rocketeer, juggernaut, Halcyon elite) and red BOSS (commander, Wraith, Bulwark, Warden, Havoc tank). Each portrait is the field's own drawing of that hostile, rendered by the game (`BPRender.portrait`), so nothing new had to be drawn or licensed.

Every file is on the shelf from the start, so the player sees how much there is to find, but a hostile never met is only its silhouette in its grade's colour, UNIDENTIFIED, with where it was last reported. A file fills in three steps:

- **Sighted**, the first time the player gets a clear look at one (in line of sight within 950 units; a cloaked one only once it shows itself): its portrait, name, role, description and where it is met (operations, Insane reinforcements, the holdout wave it joins).
- **Studied**, after the first kill: how to beat it (the same counter the death screen gives), its traits and its numbers at standard pressure.
- **Complete**, after 15 / 10 / 8 / 5 / 2 kills by grade: its weak point and what it drops, and a one-time payout of 100 / 150 / 250 / 400 / 750 CR (7,900 CR for the whole book). A banner says so in the field.

Kills by the player's squad count (not an AI teammate's); every member of a LAN squad files their own. Team deathmatch and the range file nothing. A save from before Intel opens with the files it has earned - the hostiles of each operation it cleared, Insane reinforcements if it cleared it on Insane, and those a holdout run of its best wave met - as sightings only. The service record shows the count. `tests/intel.cjs` checks the files against what the game really spawns.

## Accessibility

Settings: text size (100 / 115 / 130%, menus, HUD and labels in the field), colour palette (standard, red–green safe, blue–yellow safe, high contrast; recolours hostiles, teams and attack warnings, which also differ by shape), and hold or toggle for sprint and steady aim. Reduced flashes, screen shake and visual detail were already there. Banners queue for at least 1.4 seconds each instead of overwriting one another, and move clear of a boss bar.

## Enemies and bosses

Riflemen, rushers, marksmen, shields, drones and the commander are joined by scouts (SMG bursts), breachers (shotgun pushers), grenadiers (lobbed shells with ground rings), machine gunners (long tracking bursts), rocketeers (laser-telegraphed rockets that destroy cover), sentry guns (front-armoured, slow turn — flank or EMP them), cloaked ghosts (only visible up close), bomb drones and Halcyon elites (pulse bursts). Bosses: the **Wraith** (blinks between perches after every shot), the **Bulwark** (armoured roadblock: bullets do 12%, explosives full; mortar salvos; EMP opens a window) and the **Warden** (mech whose barrier cuts damage 92% until a generator is destroyed or it is EMP'd; stomps up close). Killing a boss or finishing a defence routs that fight's reinforcements.

**Reading a hostile at a glance:** each type has its own headgear and the dangerous ones carry a small badge (a cross for medics, a scope for marksmen, a blast mark for grenadiers, mortars and rocketeers, a hex for engineers, a signal for jammers and spotters, a diamond for heavies). The fallen go grey and flat, then fade after six seconds (only hostiles a wave sent in are removed; authored campaign soldiers stay so checkpoints know who is dead).

## Economy and retention

Credits come from kill bounties (+10% per chained kill up to +80%, ×1.5 knife, ×1.25 mid-slide, and ×0.6 in HOLDOUT where hostiles are far more numerous), mission completion, rating, flows, NO DEATHS, intel, first clears per difficulty, medals (5 per operation), daily contracts (3 rotating per day), a daily-operation streak bonus, rank-ups (150 × rank) and the Scavenger upgrade. They buy guns, gadgets, seven three-level upgrades and, once a gun's mastery allows, three kit parts per gun. The armory opens in five tiers at ranks 1, 4, 7, 10 and 15. A tier is also an item's grade, and every screen that shows a gun, gadget or upgrade colours it by that: standard issue (silver), field grade (green), specialist (blue), elite (gold) and legendary (a fiery red, for the XR-9 at 60,000 CR and the ARC-7 at 85,000 CR, which cost several times anything else and open at the last rank title). A gun dropped by an enemy can be carried for that run only; finding one makes it 25% off in the armory for good. Prices and the rank curve are paced so everything but the legendary pair takes roughly thirteen hours and the pair about eight more (`node blackout/qa/economy-report.cjs` prints the payout tables and that estimate, with its assumptions). The reasoning and the numbers are in `docs/BLACKOUT_GAMEPLAY_PLAN.md`. Each later operation is tuned around specific gear the briefing recommends: smoke and a long rifle for Dead Frequency, flashbangs for Underground, explosives for the Bulwark, EMP for Site Meridian.

Feedback: animated debrief tally, XP bar and award cards; NEW WEAPON / combo / objective banners; credit popups; hit and kill markers; slow-motion on boss kills and wave clears; damage-direction arcs; low-health red pulse with heartbeat and laboured breathing until healed; flashbang white-out and ringing; bullet whiz; per-theme ambience and weather.

## Destruction and rendering

Props own real health; destroyed props stop blocking. New walls: water and chain-link fence (block movement, not bullets or sight), gates opened by objectives, jammers/servers/generators, fuel tankers. Six themes (city, ruins at dusk, night snowfield, blacked-out subway, desert highway, lab facility) are baked into one texture per map. The subway uses a render-texture darkness layer with a flashlight cone, lamps, muzzle flashes and explosions erased out of it.
